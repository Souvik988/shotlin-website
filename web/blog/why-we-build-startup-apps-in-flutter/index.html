Why we build startup apps in Flutter (and when we don't)

One codebase, two app stores, native speed. How Flutter helps startups launch faster on Android and iOS — and the cases where we pick something else.

Key takeaways

  • One Flutter codebase ships to Android and iOS, so features stay in sync.
  • Flutter compiles to native code — smooth enough for maps, payments and live tracking.
  • We choose web, native or a game engine when a project genuinely needs it.
  • Shared code usually means less to build, test and maintain.

Bakaloo, FreshCart, LNDRY and Dealker all run on Flutter. That's not loyalty to a framework — it's what consistently gets startups to both app stores fastest. Here's our reasoning, and the cases where we pick something else. (See them in our projects.)

One codebase, two stores

A single Flutter codebase ships to Android and iOS. Features land on both platforms at the same time, bugs get fixed once, and one team owns the whole app instead of two teams drifting apart.

Fast enough for real products

Flutter compiles to native machine code and draws its own interface, so scrolling, animations and screens full of products stay smooth — including live maps, payments and order tracking.

When we pick something else

  • A web app or PWA, when the product lives in a browser or needs no store presence.
  • Fully native code, when a project depends on a platform SDK Flutter can't reach well.
  • Unity or Godot, for games — which is how we built Rustbloom.

What it means for your budget

Fewer duplicated screens, one QA pass per feature and a shared design system. For most startups that means less to build now and less to maintain later.

Frequently asked questions

Is Flutter good for startups?

For most product apps, yes. One codebase for Android and iOS speeds up launch and keeps maintenance lean.

Can a Flutter app handle payments, maps and push notifications?

Yes. Our Flutter apps use UPI and card payments, live maps, push notifications and WhatsApp integrations.

Will a Flutter app feel native?

Flutter renders its own UI at native speed. With careful design it feels at home on both platforms.
