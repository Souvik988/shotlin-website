Key takeaways

  • A grocery business usually needs four apps: customer, store, rider and admin.
  • Delivery zones, slots and inventory sync matter more than extra features.
  • Plan payments, GST invoices and refunds before launch day.
  • Run test orders in every zone before you go live.

We've shipped grocery and fresh-food delivery products like Bakaloo and FreshCart. This is the checklist we wish every founder had on day one. (Short on time? Our grocery launch kit covers most of it.)

The apps you actually need

  • Customer app: browse, search, cart, slots, payments and live tracking.
  • Store or picker app: incoming orders, picking lists and substitutions.
  • Rider app: assignments, navigation and proof of delivery.
  • Admin dashboard: catalogue, prices, offers, zones and reports.

Operations before features

Draw delivery zones carefully, decide your delivery slots, and keep inventory in sync with the shelf. A beautiful app with wrong stock creates angry customers on day one.

Payments, invoices and refunds

Support UPI, cards and cash on delivery, generate GST-ready invoices, and decide how refunds and substitutions work before the first order arrives.

Launch week

  • Store listings with real screenshots and a clear description.
  • Test orders in every delivery zone, at every time slot.
  • Rider onboarding and a WhatsApp support line for customers.

After launch

Offers for first orders, gentle nudges for repeat orders, and a dashboard that shows which products, zones and hours actually make money.

Frequently asked questions

How many apps does a grocery delivery business need?

Usually four: a customer app, a store or picker app, a rider app and an admin dashboard.

Can I start with one store?

Yes. Start with one store and one delivery zone, then add stores and zones from the admin dashboard as you grow.
