Launch kit vs custom build: which is right for your startup?

When a prebuilt app kit beats building from scratch — and when it doesn't. A founder's checklist for choosing your first product route.

Key takeaways

  • A launch kit is a proven product rebranded for you — the fastest route to market.
  • A custom build fits a unique workflow, deep integrations or special scale needs.
  • Many teams start with a kit and add custom modules once real users arrive.

Every founder hits the same fork early on: start from something that already works, or build exactly what's in your head. Both are valid. Picking the wrong one costs months.

When a launch kit wins

  • Your business model already exists — grocery delivery, a marketplace, a services booking app.
  • Speed to market matters more than a unique workflow.
  • You want to test demand before investing in custom engineering.
  • You'd rather spend budget on marketing than on rebuilding a checkout.

A good kit is not a template. It's a product that is already running for real users, with the edge cases — refunds, failed payments, rider no-shows — already handled. Browse our launch kits to see what that looks like.

When a custom build wins

  • Your core workflow is the product, and no existing app does it.
  • You need deep integrations with internal systems or hardware.
  • You're planning for a scale or compliance requirement a kit can't meet.

The hybrid most founders end up with

In practice, many of our projects start from a kit and grow custom modules on top. You launch fast, learn from real users, then invest where it actually moves the business. See our custom builds for where that road leads.
