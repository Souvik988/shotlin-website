Why we deploy every client product on servers they own

Vendor lock-in is a silent tax on startups. Here's why every Shotlin product ships on infrastructure in the client's name — and how Opslin makes it easy.

Key takeaways

  • Your code, domains, store listings and servers should be in your name.
  • Opslin makes self-hosting on your own VPS simple.
  • Owning the stack means predictable costs and no lock-in.

Too many startups discover — at the worst possible moment — that their app lives on an agency's account. Changing developers then means migrating everything.

Our rule: you own the stack

Code repositories, app store listings, domains and servers are created in your name from day one. We get access; you keep control.

Making self-hosting painless

Owning a VPS used to mean owning its problems. That's why we built Opslin: connect a Linux server you own and go live in about five minutes, with domains, SSL, databases, logs, monitoring and one-click rollback.

  • Git push to a health-checked release.
  • An outbound-only agent, so no inbound ports to expose.
  • PostgreSQL, MySQL, MongoDB and Redis managed from one place.

The result: predictable costs, no lock-in, and an exit door you never have to use.
